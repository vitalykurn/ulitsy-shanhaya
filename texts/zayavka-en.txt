## Project Description
My name is Vera Kurnosenko. I am 12, and Shanghai is home: I have lived here for about ten years. I started Chinese school in 2020. Since September 2025 I also study by distance on the Russian program, and history is part of it. In class people speak Chinese. My classmates have heard of Russia. That a piece of that history stands on our street is something almost no one thinks about.

The project is called "Russian History on the Streets of Shanghai". It is a map for my classmates. Walking them past pretty houses does not interest me. I want each house to have a story you can find in a textbook.

The Pushkin monument stands at the corner of Yueyang and Taojiang. Russians in Shanghai put it up in 1937, for one hundred years since the poet died. His face pointed north, toward Russia. Later the monument was destroyed. In 1987 they opened it again, and now he looks south. In China a respected person is often turned toward the sun. In 1937 they set him toward Russia: people needed the memory of Pushkin, not a nice view.

The cathedral on Xinle Road, house 55, is dedicated to the icon "Surety of Sinners". Emigrants built it themselves, from 1933 to 1937. At Gaolan 16 stands St. Nicholas Church. Both houses are about the same thing. After the revolution and the Civil War, Russian families lived in Shanghai and kept school, language, and church here.

Huaihai Road used to be Avenue Joffre. In the 1920s–1940s people spoke Russian there: newspapers, schools, theaters, shops. Alexander Vertinsky performed. I understand that without a tour guide. I switch between Chinese and Russian every day myself.

About 1950 I was wrong at first. I thought Soviet pilots were in Shanghai because of the war in Korea. That is not true. In February 1950 an air-defense group came to the city. Lieutenant General Pavel Batitsky was in command; Lieutenant General Sidor Slyusarev led the pilots. They covered Shanghai from air raids. By autumn the work was finished. Korea was somewhere else: bases in northeast China. I put the Shanghai Exhibition Center on Yan'an Road next to this only because it is also from the 1950s. It was built in 1955 as the House of Sino-Soviet Friendship. The building has nothing to do with the pilots. The city still holds exhibitions there.

The map grew out of ordinary days. Since 2023 I have been in a Chinese class and many times explained who I am and where I come from. Since 2025 history lessons from Moscow were added. I will tell the stories in Russian and Chinese, with photos from the sidewalk and a presentation. At school I already make presentations like that.

## Project relevance
Classmates walk past the Pushkin monument and old Russian houses and see only Shanghai. I did not understand at once that this was our history either: books, families after the Civil War, a language they did not drop, and the pilots of 1950.

There are books for adults. A story for my age, and in both Russian and Chinese, I could not find. I am 12, I grew up here, and I can say this to friends in their language, right on their street.

I need it for myself too. When you live outside Russia, history easily stays a lesson on a screen. I am in grade 7 of the international division at Jincai School, in Pudong, and I also follow the Russian program. The map joins the textbook and the city I walk to school in.

There is a place where it is easy to get confused. People sometimes say the pilots in Shanghai were about Korea. I thought that myself. In 1950 an air-defense group protected the city itself. The war in Korea ran from bases in northeast China. If I say that clearly in class, classmates will at least not repeat my mistake.

## Originality of the project concept
An adult usually tells the story of Russian Shanghai. Mine is different, and it comes from my life, not from a guidebook.

I grew up here. I went to Yangjing Juyuan School, then studied in Jing'an near the old French Concession, and now I am in Pudong. The pins on the map sit between those districts. I did not dig them up in one evening.

The listeners will be kids from a Chinese class. At Jincai we often speak Chinese at the board. In summer 2026 I made a presentation about holidays in Dalian. I can talk about Russian history the same way: like at recess, not like an encyclopedia.

I chose places by asking what Russia is doing there. With Pushkin it is literature. With the cathedral and Huaihai it is life after the Civil War. With 1950 it is Batitsky and Slyusarev. I peel Korea away from them because I mixed it all up myself at first. The 1955 exhibition center I keep as a separate story. It is a friendship building, not an airfield.

## Scale of project implementation
The project runs in Shanghai. The audience is children who live here.

First my class: grade 7 of the international division at Jincai School. I will tell it in a lesson, in Chinese and Russian. Then distance classes. Since 19 September 2025 I study at the International School of Tomorrow in Moscow. On their program I am now in grade 6, history is on the schedule, and I can show the map there too.

The houses themselves stand in Xuhui and Huangpu. I live in Pudong and used to study in Jing'an. The old center, where the Russian houses remain, thus links to the districts where we go to school now.

There will be an oral talk, a presentation, photos of four places, and short captions in two languages. The presentation, the website, and the photo folder open by the links in this application. Newspapers have not written about the project.

Later I want to show the route to one more class and to Russian kids in Shanghai. For now there is one author and one school. I am twelve. I am not promising more yet.

## Educational significance of the project
I want friends to be able to retell four stories, not one vague line about Russia.

Pushkin. Russians in Shanghai put up a monument to the poet and first turned his face toward Russia. They needed the book, not street decoration.

The cathedral and Huaihai. After the revolution and the Civil War, families lived here and did not drop language, school, or faith. That is Russian culture. It just happened in China.

1950. Soviet pilots and anti-aircraft crews defended the city. The commanders are Pavel Batitsky and Sidor Slyusarev. This is not the war in Korea. I already mixed those two stories, so in the talk I keep them apart on purpose.

I need the map so lessons from Moscow stand on a familiar street. Classmates need it so Russia is the city of their friend, not only a country from the news.
